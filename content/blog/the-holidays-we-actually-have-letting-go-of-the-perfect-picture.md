I've been thinking about how much pressure we sometimes place on ourselves during the holidays.

We want things to feel special. We want people to be happy. We want to create meaningful memories.

And sometimes, without realizing it, we start trying to make everything look and feel a certain way.

The gathering should go smoothly. The traditions should continue. Everyone should get along. We should feel grateful, joyful, and excited.

But life doesn't always follow the picture we've created in our minds.

I'm learning that there's a difference between making something meaningful and trying to make it perfect.

## Not Everyone Experiences the Holidays the Same Way

For some people, this season brings celebrations, family gatherings, familiar traditions, and time away from work.

For others, it means working holiday shifts, caring for someone who needs ongoing assistance, navigating difficult family relationships, or spending the season away from people they love.

Some are grieving.

Some are worried about finances.

Some are simply tired.

And some may not celebrate a particular holiday at all.

I think it's important to make room for those different experiences.

We don't need to compare our holidays with anyone else's, and we don't need to force ourselves to feel something we're not feeling.

## When Expectations Become Heavy

Something I've been reflecting on is how quickly our desire to make others happy can turn into feeling responsible for their entire experience.

We can plan a gathering, prepare a meal, arrange an activity, or make time for someone we care about.

But we can't guarantee that everyone will enjoy themselves, agree with one another, or respond the way we'd like.

That doesn't mean we stop caring.

It means recognizing where our responsibilities end and where other people's choices and feelings begin.

And in situations where someone depends on us for care, our responsibilities may be substantial. We still have to meet those needs and take appropriate action when safety is involved.

But even then, we don't have to hold ourselves responsible for every emotion or outcome.

## A Few Ways to Make the Season More Meaningful

I've been considering some small changes that might help us spend less energy on expectations and more on what matters.

**Keep one tradition that genuinely brings joy.** We don't have to continue every tradition simply because we've always done it.**Ask for help or share a responsibility.** Preparing for a gathering or supporting others doesn't have to fall entirely on one person.**Create a quiet moment.** A short walk, a favorite song, or a few minutes sitting with a warm drink may offer a welcome break from the activity around us.**Reach out to someone who might feel alone.** A simple message or conversation can be meaningful without requiring a grand gesture.**Allow the day to be what it is.** Some moments may be wonderful. Others may be difficult. We can acknowledge both without deciding the entire holiday has been ruined.

These aren't rules for creating a better holiday. They're possibilities worth considering.

## A Holiday Journaling Moment

If you have a few minutes this week, consider these questions:

1.  What am I hoping this holiday season will look or feel like?

2.  Which expectations are meaningful to me, and which are creating unnecessary pressure?

3.  Am I trying to manage someone else's feelings or experience?

4.  What is one small tradition, connection, or activity I'd genuinely like to enjoy?

5.  How can I make room for my own needs while honoring responsibilities that truly require my attention?

You might also complete this sentence:

*This holiday season, I want to spend less energy on \_\_\_\_\_\_ and more time appreciating \_\_\_\_\_\_.*

## Making Room for What Matters

At ThriveWell Care Collective™, we talk about the value of pausing, noticing what we need, and creating opportunities for restoration.

For me, that includes learning to appreciate the moments that are actually happening instead of constantly comparing them with how I thought they should happen.

Maybe the meal isn't perfect.

Maybe someone can't attend.

Maybe plans change.

Or perhaps this year looks entirely different from previous years.

That doesn't mean there can't still be moments of meaning, kindness, connection, or rest.

**One thought to carry into your week:**

*What would make this season more meaningful and less pressured?*

However you're spending the holidays, I hope you find room to experience them in a way that feels right for you.

And if you're caring for others, I hope you remember that your experience matters, too.
