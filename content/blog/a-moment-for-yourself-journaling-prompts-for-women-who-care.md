**When was the last time you stopped and asked yourself, "How am I doing?"**

It's a simple question, but when you're busy taking care of everyone else, it can be surprisingly easy to forget to ask.

Maybe you're juggling work and family, checking in on an aging parent, raising children, or making sure everyone on your team has what they need. By the end of the day, you've probably asked a dozen people how they're doing. But what about you?

At ThriveWell Care Collective, we believe taking care of yourself doesn't have to be complicated. Sometimes, it's as simple as finding a quiet moment, picking up a notebook, and giving yourself a chance to think about what's on your mind.

## Why Give Journaling a Try?

Have you ever had so much going on in your head that you didn't know where to begin?

Sometimes, putting those thoughts on paper can help you sort through them. Other times, you might discover something you hadn't realized you were feeling.

And some days, you might just need a place to let it all out.

You don't have to be a good writer. You don't need a beautiful journal or a quiet hour to yourself. A few sentences scribbled in a notebook can be enough.

It's not about writing something meaningful or finding the perfect words. It's about giving yourself a little attention, too.

## Four Simple Prompts to Get You Started

At ThriveWell, we use a four-step approach called **Arrive • Notice • Release • Return™** to help women pause, reconnect, and make space for themselves.

You can use these same four steps when journaling.

### Arrive — Take a Moment to Settle In

Before you start writing, take a comfortable breath and notice how you're feeling.

Maybe you're tired, distracted, overwhelmed, or feeling pretty good today. Whatever it is, there's no need to change it.

**Try writing about:**

*What am I bringing into this moment, and what would help me feel a little more present?*

### Notice — Check In With Yourself

We're often so tuned in to what everyone else needs that we overlook our own.

Think about how you've been feeling lately. Is there something you've been putting off for yourself? Something you've needed but haven't made time for?

**Try writing about:**

*I've been paying attention to everyone else's needs. What have I been needing lately?*

### Release — Put Something Down for a While

Some days, we carry more than we realize. Responsibilities, worries, expectations, and the feeling that we should somehow be doing more.

You don't have to solve everything today. And you don't have to carry every responsibility by yourself.

**Try writing about:**

*What am I carrying today that I can give myself permission to set aside?*

### Return — Take Something Good With You

Before closing your journal, think about one small thing you could do for yourself.

It doesn't have to be anything big. Maybe it's stepping outside for a few minutes, calling someone who makes you laugh, or simply allowing yourself to rest without feeling guilty.

**Try writing about:**

*What is one small act of kindness or care I can offer myself today or this week?*

## Make Journaling Work for You

There's no right time or right way to journal.

Maybe you enjoy writing with your morning coffee. Maybe a few minutes before bed works better. Or maybe you only reach for your notebook when something is weighing on your mind.

All of those are perfectly fine.

You can also return to these same questions again and again. Your answers might look different next week, next month, or even tomorrow.

And if you don't feel like writing one day? That's okay, too.

The last thing any of us needs is another item on our to-do list.

## A Little Care for Yourself Goes a Long Way

When you're the person others depend on, it's easy to feel as though your own needs can wait.

But you matter in the middle of all that caring, too.

At ThriveWell, we want to help women find small, realistic ways to pause, restore, and reconnect with themselves. Journaling is just one of those ways.

So, if you have a few minutes today, find a comfortable spot, grab a notebook, and choose one question.

You don't need to have the answers. Just start wherever you are.
