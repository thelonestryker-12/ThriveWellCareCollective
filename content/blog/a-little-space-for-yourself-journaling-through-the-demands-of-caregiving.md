**When you're busy caring for everyone else, it's easy to forget to check in with yourself.**

Maybe your day begins with getting the kids ready, checking on a parent, answering work messages, or thinking about everything that needs to get done. Before you know it, the day is over, and you haven't had a moment to consider how you're feeling.

Sound familiar?

Whether you're caring for family, supporting others professionally, or leading a team, there's often a lot on your mind. And sometimes, you just need somewhere to put it all.

That's where journaling can help.

At ThriveWell Care Collective, we believe self-care doesn't have to mean finding an extra hour in your day. Sometimes, it's five quiet minutes, a notebook, and the freedom to write whatever comes to mind.

## Five Simple Ways to Make Journaling Your Own

You don't have to write pages or follow a particular routine. Some days, a few sentences might be enough. Other days, you may have more to say.

Here are five ways to get started, depending on what you need.

### 1. Check In With Yourself

When was the last time someone asked how you were doing—and you gave yourself a moment to think about the answer?

Take two minutes and finish these thoughts:

- Today, I'm feeling...
- Something on my mind is...
- Right now, I could really use...

You don't have to fix anything or figure out what comes next. Just give yourself permission to acknowledge how you're doing.

### 2. Get It All Out of Your Head

You know those days when your mind feels like it has 27 tabs open?

There's the grocery list, tomorrow's appointments, a work deadline, something you forgot to do, and that conversation you keep replaying.

Instead of trying to keep track of everything, write it down.

Don't worry about spelling, complete sentences, or making it organized. Just get your thoughts onto paper.

Then ask yourself:

*What really needs my attention today, and what can wait?*

Sometimes, seeing everything written down helps you decide where to begin.

### 3. Be a Little Kinder to Yourself

Caregivers are often quick to offer patience and understanding to others. But when it comes to ourselves? We can be pretty tough critics.

Maybe you didn't get everything done today. Maybe you lost your patience. Maybe you're simply tired.

Try asking yourself:

- Am I expecting too much of myself right now?
- What would I tell a friend who was having a day like mine?
- What's one thing I did well today, even if nobody noticed?

You deserve some of the same kindness you give so freely to others.

### 4. Think About Your Boundaries

Have you ever said yes to something when you were already exhausted?

Many of us have. We want to help, we don't want to disappoint anyone, and sometimes it's easier to say yes than explain why we can't.

But your time and energy matter, too.

Consider writing about:

- Where am I feeling stretched too thin?
- What am I doing that someone else could help with?
- What's one thing I could say no to without feeling that I have to explain myself?

Boundaries don't always require big conversations or major changes. Sometimes, they begin with recognizing that you need a little breathing room.

### 5. Notice the Little Things

Not every day is going to be a good day. Some days are just hard.

But even on those days, there may be small moments worth remembering.

Maybe someone made you laugh. You enjoyed your coffee while it was still hot. You stepped outside and felt the sunshine. Or you finally sat down after a long day.

Before going to bed, ask yourself:

- What made me smile today?
- Was there a moment when I felt a little more relaxed?
- What's something small I'd like to make time for tomorrow?

This isn't about forcing yourself to be positive. It's simply about noticing moments that might otherwise get lost in a busy day.

## What If Journaling Just Isn't Your Thing?

That's perfectly fine.

You might prefer making a quick list instead of writing paragraphs. Maybe you only journal once a week, or when something is bothering you. You might even start by writing, "I have no idea what to write about."

That counts.

There's no perfect journal, no required number of pages, and no schedule you have to follow.

And if you miss a few days—or a few weeks—you can simply pick up where you left off.

## Making a Little Room for Yourself

At ThriveWell, our **Arrive • Notice • Release • Return™** approach is about creating space to become present, recognize what you need, let go of some of what you're carrying, and reconnect with yourself.

Journaling fits naturally into that process.

You might write a few thoughts after a massage, spend five minutes reflecting before bed, or keep a notebook nearby for those moments when you need to clear your head.

The goal isn't to add one more responsibility to your day. It's to give yourself a place where, for a few moments, the focus can be on you.

## You Deserve a Moment, Too

When you spend so much of your time looking after others, it's easy to put your own needs at the bottom of the list.

But your thoughts, feelings, and experiences deserve attention, too.

So, the next time you find yourself with a few quiet minutes, grab a notebook and see what comes to mind.

You might be surprised by what you have to say when you give yourself the chance to listen.
