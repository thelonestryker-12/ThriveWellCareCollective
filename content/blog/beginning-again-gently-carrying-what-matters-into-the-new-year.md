There's something about the end of December that makes me want to look back.

I find myself thinking about what happened during the year, what went well, what didn't turn out as expected, and what I might want to do differently.

Some years bring exciting opportunities and meaningful accomplishments. Others bring unexpected challenges, difficult decisions, or changes we didn't see coming.

And sometimes we experience a little of everything.

I've been learning that reflecting on the past doesn't have to mean judging myself for how everything turned out.

## Looking Back Without Keeping Score

As the year ends, we're often encouraged to evaluate our progress.

What did we accomplish? What goals did we reach? What should we have done differently?

There's value in reflecting on those questions, but I've been trying to approach them with a little more kindness.

Maybe I handled something better than I would have a year ago.

Maybe I recognized a pattern that had been affecting me for a long time.

Maybe I finally asked for help, set a boundary, or stopped trying to manage something that was never entirely within my control.

Those changes may not appear on a list of traditional accomplishments.

But they matter.

And sometimes what we learn from a difficult experience is just as meaningful as what we accomplish.

## What Am I Ready to Carry Forward?

I've also been thinking about the difference between learning from the past and continuing to carry it.

We can't change what has already happened.

There may be decisions we wish we'd made differently, conversations we'd like to revisit, or situations that still feel unresolved.

But we can consider what those experiences have taught us.

Perhaps we've learned something about our limits.

Perhaps we're beginning to recognize when we need support.

Or maybe we're learning to spend less energy trying to influence other people's choices and more energy making thoughtful decisions about our own lives.

For me, that feels like a meaningful way to approach a new year.

Not by pretending the past didn't happen, but by deciding what lessons are worth carrying forward.

## A New Year Doesn't Require a New You

I sometimes find the pressure surrounding New Year's resolutions a little overwhelming.

We're encouraged to set ambitious goals, develop new routines, improve ourselves, and begin January with renewed motivation.

There's nothing wrong with wanting to make changes.

But I don't think we need to completely reinvent ourselves each January.

Sometimes meaningful change begins with something much smaller.

Recognizing when we're tired.

Asking for help.

Taking time to reflect before responding.

Making room for a relationship that matters.

Or accepting that some situations may take longer to unfold than we'd like.

These aren't necessarily dramatic changes. But they can influence how we experience everyday life.

## A Year-End Journaling Moment

Before turning the calendar to January, consider spending a little time with these questions:

1.  What did this year teach me about myself?

2.  What am I proud of that I might otherwise overlook?

3.  What experience or expectation am I ready to let go of as I enter the new year?

4.  Where have I become more aware of my own needs, boundaries, or capacity?

5.  What is one small choice I can make in January that reflects the person I want to be?

You might also try completing these thoughts:

*Something I'm grateful for is...*

*Something I'm still learning is...*

*Something I'd like to make more room for is...*

There's no need to turn your answers into a long list of resolutions.

Let them simply be reflections.

## Moving Forward With Greater Awareness

One thing I'm learning is that I don't need to have everything figured out to move forward.

I can make decisions based on what I know today.

I can ask for guidance or support when I need it.

I can reconsider something when new information becomes available.

And I can recognize that progress doesn't always follow a straight line.

At ThriveWell Care Collective™, our Arrive • Notice • Release • Return™ approach reminds us that we can pause, pay attention to what we're experiencing, make room for what matters, and return to our lives with greater awareness.

As this year comes to a close, that's something I'd like to carry with me.

Not the pressure to become a completely different person.

Just the willingness to keep learning.

## A Thought for the New Year

**What small choice do I want to carry into the new year?**

Maybe it's making a little more time for yourself.

Maybe it's asking for support rather than managing everything alone.

Or perhaps it's simply remembering that you don't have to know exactly what comes next.

Whatever the answer, I hope you approach the new year with curiosity, kindness, and a little more room for yourself.
