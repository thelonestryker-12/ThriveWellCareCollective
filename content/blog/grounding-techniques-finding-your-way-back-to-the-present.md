Life can feel overwhelming, especially when you're caring for others, managing responsibilities, or navigating a busy day. Sometimes, even a few moments of intentional awareness can help you feel more centered.

Grounding techniques offer a simple way to reconnect with yourself and the present moment.

## What Is Grounding?

Grounding is a practice that helps redirect your attention away from overwhelming thoughts or emotions and toward what is happening around you.

Grounding techniques use your senses, thoughts, or physical awareness to bring your attention back to the present. These practices can help manage stress and anxiety and support emotional awareness (Langer, 2025).

## Why Practice Grounding?

When life becomes demanding, it's easy to move through the day without noticing how you're feeling.

Grounding creates an opportunity to:

- Reconnect with the present moment.
- Shift attention away from stressful thoughts.
- Notice physical sensations and surroundings.
- Create a brief moment of calm.
- Respond to situations with greater awareness.

Grounding doesn't eliminate life's challenges. Instead, it offers a practical way to pause and reconnect before moving forward.

## Try This: A Simple Sensory Grounding Practice

You don't need special equipment or a quiet room. This exercise can be practiced almost anywhere.

**Arrive:** Find a comfortable position. Allow yourself a moment to settle.

**Notice:** Gently bring your attention to your surroundings.

- Identify **3 things you can see.**
- Notice **2 things you can physically feel**, such as your feet on the floor or the fabric of your clothing.
- Listen for **1 sound** around you.

**Release:** Allow your attention to rest on what you're noticing rather than what you need to accomplish next.

**Return:** Take a comfortable breath and gently return to your day.

*This simplified exercise is adapted from the 5-4-3-2-1 sensory grounding technique described by Public Health Scotland (2025).*

## Small Moments Make a Difference

Grounding doesn't require a lengthy routine. You might practice before a meeting, between caregiving responsibilities, or whenever you notice yourself feeling overwhelmed.

The goal isn't to feel perfectly calm every time. It's to create a moment of awareness and permit yourself to reconnect.

At **ThriveWell Care Collective™**, we believe restoration can begin with small, intentional moments. Through our **Arrive • Notice • Release • Return™** approach, we encourage women to make space for their own well-being while continuing to care for the people and responsibilities that matter to them.

**Sometimes, reconnecting with yourself begins by simply noticing where you are.**
