I've spent much of my life in roles that involve caring for, supporting, teaching, and helping others. Whether in my professional work, with family, or in my community, I've often been the person who tries to figure things out, find solutions, and keep things moving forward.

And for a long time, I didn't give much thought to how much energy that takes.

When you're used to being the person others depend on, it's easy to overlook your own needs. You tell yourself you'll slow down when things settle down, when everyone else is okay, or when you finally get through everything on your list.

But I've come to realize that there will always be another responsibility, another concern, or someone who needs something.

At some point, I had to ask myself a question I hadn't been asking often enough: **What do I need?**

## Learning to Pay Attention

One thing I've been learning is that taking care of myself begins with being honest about how I'm doing.

Not how I think I should be doing. Not the answer I give when someone asks, and I automatically say, "I'm fine."

How am I really doing?

Sometimes the answer is that I'm tired. Sometimes I'm worried about something I can't change. Other times, I realize I've taken on responsibilities that were never really mine to carry.

I've also learned that I don't have to turn every feeling into a problem that needs to be solved.

Sometimes I just need to recognize it.

## Recognizing What I Can—and Can't—Control

This has been an important part of my own reflection.

I've spent plenty of time worrying about outcomes, anticipating problems, and trying to make things easier for everyone around me.

But I've been learning to ask myself whether something is actually within my control.

Can I change the situation, or can I only choose how I respond to it?

Am I helping because someone needs my help, or because I feel responsible for making everything work out?

Those aren't always comfortable questions. But they're worthwhile ones.

I'm discovering that caring about someone doesn't mean I have to carry everything for them. And stepping back doesn't mean I care any less.

## Making Room for What Matters

I've also started thinking differently about what it means to take care of myself.

It doesn't always mean scheduling something special or finding an entire afternoon to relax.

Sometimes it means saying no when I don't have the capacity to say yes.

Sometimes it means recognizing that I've done enough for the day.

And sometimes it means taking a few quiet minutes to appreciate something good instead of immediately moving on to the next thing.

I'm learning that rest doesn't have to be earned by finishing everything first.

That's still something I have to remind myself.

## Why Reflection Matters to Me

Writing things down has become one way to sort through what's on my mind.

It allows me to notice patterns, question my assumptions, acknowledge what I'm feeling, and think about what I might want to do differently.

Some days, reflection helps me recognize something I'm grateful for. Other days, it helps me see where I need a boundary, a different perspective, or a little more patience with myself.

And sometimes, I don't come away with an answer at all.

I'm learning to be okay with that, too.

## Why We're Starting This Series

These experiences are part of what makes ThriveWell Care Collective's mission so meaningful to me.

Our **Arrive • Notice • Release • Return™** approach reflects something I continue to practice in my own life: being present, paying attention to what I need, making room to let go of what isn't serving me, and moving forward with greater awareness.

I wanted to create this reflection series because I know I'm not the only woman who sometimes puts herself at the bottom of the list.

Whether you're caring for family, supporting others professionally, raising children, leading a team, or simply managing everything life brings your way, I hope these articles give you a reason to pause and check in with yourself.

We'll explore journaling, reflection, boundaries, gratitude, and other simple ways to become more aware of our own needs.

Not because there's something we need to fix about ourselves, but because we deserve the same consideration we so readily extend to others.

## One Question to Begin

As we begin this series, I'd like to leave you with a question I've been asking myself:

**What am I carrying right now that I may not need to carry?**

You don't have to answer it immediately. You might think about it over your morning coffee, write a few thoughts in a journal, or simply let the question sit with you for a while.

I'm still learning, too.

And perhaps that's the point. We don't have to have everything figured out to begin taking better care of ourselves.

Sometimes, we just have to make a little room.
