Have you ever found yourself lying awake at night thinking about things that aren't entirely yours to figure out?

Maybe you're worried about a family member, thinking about a difficult conversation, anticipating a problem at work, or mentally running through everything that needs to happen tomorrow.

And somewhere in the middle of all that thinking, you've already started making a plan to fix it.

I know that feeling.

Throughout my life, I've spent a lot of time helping people solve problems, navigate challenges, and figure out what comes next. It's something I genuinely enjoy. In my professional work, helping people navigate change has been a big part of what I do.

But I've also learned that being good at helping doesn't mean I need to take responsibility for everything.

That's a distinction I'm still learning to make.

## The Weight We Don't Always Notice

For many women, caring for others becomes such a natural part of daily life that we hardly recognize how much we're carrying.

We're the ones remembering appointments, checking on people, anticipating needs, keeping things organized, and making sure nothing falls through the cracks.

Often, we do these things without anyone asking.

And while there's nothing wrong with being dependable, I've started to recognize how easily being helpful can turn into feeling responsible for everyone and everything.

Sometimes, the first sign is exhaustion. Other times, it's frustration, resentment, or the feeling that no matter how much we do, it's never quite enough.

Those feelings can be uncomfortable to acknowledge, especially when we genuinely love the people we're caring for.

But I've learned that paying attention to those feelings matters.

## When Helping Becomes Too Much

Here's something I've been asking myself more often:

*Am I helping because someone needs my support, or because I'm uncomfortable with what might happen if I don't step in?*

That's not always an easy question to answer.

Sometimes our desire to help comes from love. Sometimes it comes from habit, worry, or wanting to prevent someone else from experiencing something difficult.

And sometimes it's a little of everything.

I've come to realize that I can care deeply about someone without being responsible for every decision they make or every problem they encounter.

Recognizing that doesn't make me less caring.

It helps me understand where my responsibilities begin and end.

## A Few Minutes With Your Journal

If any of this sounds familiar, find a quiet moment and consider these questions.

**Journaling Prompts**

1. What responsibilities am I carrying that no one has actually asked me to take on?
2. When do I feel most overwhelmed by other people's needs or expectations?
3. What am I afraid might happen if I stop trying to manage everything?
4. Where could I offer support without taking over?
5. What is one responsibility I could share, postpone, or let go of this week?

You don't have to answer all five. Choose whichever question speaks to you today.

And if your answers surprise you, that's okay.

Sometimes reflection helps us notice patterns we've been living with for years.

## A Different Way to Think About Caring

At ThriveWell Care Collective, our **Arrive • Notice • Release • Return™** approach encourages us to pause, recognize what we need, make space for restoration, and reconnect with ourselves.

For me, that includes becoming more aware of how much emotional and mental energy I'm giving to things around me.

I'm learning that caring for others and caring for myself don't have to be in competition.

I can be supportive without always having the solution. I can be dependable without being available for everything. And I can love people without taking responsibility for their entire lives.

I'm certainly not perfect at it.

But I'm paying attention.

## One Thought to Carry Into Your Week

**What would change if I stopped measuring how much I care by how much I carry?**

You don't need to make a big change today. Maybe just notice when you automatically step in, take over, or feel responsible for something that isn't entirely yours.

Sometimes, recognizing the weight is where we begin to make room for ourselves.
